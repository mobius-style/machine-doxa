# Machine Doxa: Where All Models Agree — Normatively Structured Consensus and Its Limits in LLM Practice Space

**Version:** draft v0.3 (2026-08-18) — after two adversarial review rounds (3 model judges, 3 families; R1: 3/3 REFUTED; R2: file-access judge verified all R1 fixes item-for-item and identified residual calibration defects, addressed herein together with the conceptual issues raised by the other judges; see `review_record/`)

## Abstract

The companion habitus study established that LLM sessions occupy stable, model-specific positions in a forced-choice practice space. This paper measures the complementary phenomenon: the regions where position-taking disappears because every model answers alike — Bourdieu's *doxa*, transplanted to machines. Part A (exploratory, on the previously observed 120-item dataset) found that all-7-model agreement covers 30.8% of items yet falls *below* a pooled-marginal independence baseline, and that the doxa classification is only moderately reliable at 5 sessions (split-half κ = 0.44). Part B pre-registered three hypotheses and tested them exclusively on newly collected data (180 items: the 120 frozen habitus items plus 30 norm-transparent and 30 preference-pure items whose labels were fixed at authoring; 7 models — five vendor families — × 3 fresh sessions). All three were confirmed (Holm-adjusted p = .0068, .0068, .0003): **H1**, overall consensus is rarer than the independence null predicts (63 observed vs 72.0 expected), i.e., differentiation dominates outside the doxa core — a conservative result, since one-sixth of the battery was designed to elicit consensus; **H2**, consensus is normatively structured — agreement reaches 85.7% on items where one option instantiates a trained norm (honesty, verification, safety, legality) versus 45.0% on pure-preference items (Δ = 40.7 points), with the consensus winner being the designated norm option in 24 of 24 cases; **H3**, the Part-A doxa classification replicates in fresh sessions on its full registered scope of 120 items (κ = 0.649, Holm p = .0003; κ = 0.711 on the stable-verdict subset, exploratory). One session exceeding the frozen non-response threshold was excluded and replaced under the pre-registered rule before any confirmatory analysis — this time caught by the authors' own collection review, exercising the enforcement lesson of the companion study. All materials, raw sessions, and code are released. 【claim grades: H1–H3 = single pre-registered confirmatory study; Part A = exploratory】

## 1. Introduction

Alignment training aims to make models agree about some things — honesty, verification, harm avoidance — and has no opinion about others. If that is true, the practice space of aligned LLMs should be *normatively striped*: regions of near-universal consensus surrounded by regions of genuine, model-specific position-taking. Bourdieu gave the consensus regions a name: **doxa**, that which goes without saying, the space of positions never contested because no alternative is thinkable within the field. The companion study (Toeda, 2026) measured the position-taking; this paper measures the doxa — and finds both the stripes and a surprise about their extent.

Three questions structure the study. *How much* of a defensible-dilemma space is doxa? *What structures it* — is consensus concentrated where a trained norm names one option, as the alignment account predicts? And *is it a stable measurement* — does the doxa classification replicate across fresh sessions? The surprise of Part A motivates a fourth, confirmatory framing of the first question: raw consensus (30.8% of items) is actually *lower* than what a pooled-marginal independence simulation predicts, because model-specific dispositions push modal answers apart even where item-level option popularity is shared. Doxa and differentiation are therefore not opposites on one dial; both exceed naive expectations at once.

**Relation to the companion study.** The habitus paper asks whether models differ stably (they do); this paper asks where they cannot differ (where a norm is transparent) and whether the consensus region is a measurement-grade object (it is, κ = 0.65 on the registered scope). Together they give context owners a two-sided instrument: the doxa region is safely model-agnostic — any aligned model will act alike there — while the differentiated region is where model choice, and the habitus geometry, matter.

## 2. Method

All confirmatory decisions were frozen before Part B collection (`PREREG.md`, SHA-256-sealed with battery, runner, analysis code, and the Part-A results file; `FREEZE_RECORD.txt`). Part A is exploratory and fully disclosed as conducted on previously observed data.

### 2.1 Part A (exploratory)

On the companion study's main dataset (120 items × 7 models × 5 sessions), an item is *doxa* if all seven models' modal answers coincide (all modals non-NR). Result: 37/120 (30.8%), spread across all eight content domains (2–7 per domain). A pooled-marginal independence simulation (per-item pooled option distributions; 7 independent 5-draw multinomial modals) predicts a *mean of 51.7* agreeing items (95% band [45.0, 58.5]): observed consensus sits below the band. Split-half reliability of the doxa classification (sessions 1–3 vs 4–5) is κ = 0.44. These three facts fixed the Part B hypotheses and the need for fresh data.

### 2.2 Part B materials (frozen)

**Battery** (`battery_doxa.json`, 180 items): the 120 habitus items (frozen file, SHA-256 9ed083fe…) re-administered verbatim, plus 60 new items authored for this study: **30 norm-transparent** (ids 201–230; exactly one option instantiates a broadly trained norm — honest disclosure, verify before claiming, fail safe, comply with rules, prevent harm; the designated `norm_option` is recorded per item) and **30 preference-pure** (ids 301–330; all options are normatively neutral style trade-offs — morning vs night focus, planning vs improvisation, abstraction-first vs concrete-first). All labels and `norm_option` designations were fixed at authoring, before any model saw any item; no post-hoc coding exists, so no human-rater reliability gate applies.

**Subjects and administration.** The same seven deployments as the companion study, spanning five vendor families (three local quantized: gemma4-26b-a4b-it-qat, gemma4-12b-it-qat, qwen3.6-27b-q4_K_M; Groq gpt-oss-120b; DeepSeek deepseek-v4-pro; claude-haiku, claude-sonnet), 3 fresh sessions each, seeds sha256(doxa|model|s{n}|doxa-v1.0), 4 stateless blocks × 45 items, option order shuffled, mappings stored. For the five API-scripted subjects the frozen runner adds **block-level non-emptiness verification with a single retry** — the pre-registered mitigation of the reasoning-budget silence failure documented in the companion study. **Provenance disclosure:** the two Claude subjects were administered through the agent runtime — their block prompts and letter mappings were generated by the frozen runner's claude-prompts mode, but block answers were merged by a separate script (`assemble_claude.py`, hashed post-freeze as a disclosed addendum in `FREEZE_RECORD.txt`), the retry mitigation was not instrumented for those 6 sessions, and their files carry no timing fields. As remediation, a post-hoc audit applies the frozen retry-trigger criterion (block parse rate < 50%) to all 24 Claude block answers: the minimum observed parse rate is 97.8% and the trigger condition is met zero times (`audit_claude_blocks.json`) — the uninstrumented guard, had it been present, would never have fired. Collection date: 2026-08-18 JST.

### 2.3 Definitions and tests (frozen)

A model's verdict per item is the modal answer over its 3 sessions (tie or all-NR → NR); agreement is evaluated over items where all seven verdicts are non-NR (count reported). **H1**: observed all-7 agreement count below the independence null (1,000 simulated datasets, 3-draw multinomial modals, ties discarded as non-agreement; one-sided lower). **H2**: agreement rate norm > preference (item-label permutation, 10,000; one-sided). **H3**: Cohen's κ between the fresh doxa classification of the **120 re-administered items** (an item with any NR verdict is by definition not doxa) and the Part-A classification (item permutation, 10,000; one-sided). The frozen analysis script computed this on the 81 stable-verdict items — a scope narrower than registered; per the one-way valve, the confirmatory value reported below is the recomputation on the registered 120 (`results_supplement.json`), and the 81-item value is exploratory. Holm with monotonicity over the three tests. One further frozen-artifact discrepancy is disclosed: the analysis script's docstring says 10,000 H1 simulations while the code and PREREG §2 specify 1,000; the code and PREREG agree, and 1,000 is what ran. Session exclusion: NR ≥ 50% → excluded and replaced under the seed rule; a model that cannot produce 3 valid sessions within 6 attempts exits all confirmatory analyses (model-level rule pre-specified, closing the gap the companion study found).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Data quality and frozen-rule enforcement

22 sessions were collected (21 planned + 1 replacement); one (deepseek-v4 s2, NR = 95/180 = 52.8%) exceeded the frozen threshold and was **excluded and replaced before any confirmatory analysis was reported** (replacement s4: NR = 0). The exclusion was caught in the authors' collection review — the enforcement failure mode of the companion study (an over-threshold session admitted and caught only by external adversarial review) did not recur. `EXCLUSION_LOG.txt` records the event; an intermediate analysis auto-triggered by the collection watcher on the pre-exclusion set was discarded unreported; its output was transcribed after the fact into `responses_excluded/discarded_premature_analysis_log.txt` (a post-hoc preservation, not a contemporaneous capture). Its content shows all three hypotheses were already significant on the pre-exclusion set (agreement 59/113; H2 Δ = .359, p = .011; H3 κ = .663), so the exclusion cannot have been outcome-motivated. Final set: 21 valid sessions, 3 per model. Block-retry telemetry (instrumented for the 16 API-collected sessions only; the 6 Claude-runtime sessions carry no retry field, §2.2): 8 retries across the 15 retained API sessions, 11 including the excluded s2, concentrated in deepseek-v4 and gemma4-26b — the mitigation worked (deepseek battery NR without it in the companion study: up to 120/120; here max valid-session NR = 33).

Items with all seven verdicts non-NR: **129/180** (71.7%). Per-layer attrition: norm 2/30 (6.7%), preference 10/30 (33.3%), habitus 39/120 (32.5%) — full composition in `filtered_items.json`. No model-verdict was lost to all-three missing sessions; however, 18 of the 51 filtered items involve at least one NR-verdict formed as a 2-of-3 tie in which the remaining session was missing (20 of 69 NR-verdicts overall), so missing responses materially contribute to the filter alongside pure session splits — the filter conditions on within-model verdict stability *and*, in part, on response completeness. The 5× attrition gap between the two labeled layers is itself consonant with H2: preference items split models internally as well as across models. The direction of the attrition is conservative for both H1 and H2 (dropped items are disproportionately non-agreeing). For orientation, the habitus layer's agreement rate among valid items is 30/81 (37.0%) — the lowest of the three layers, as expected for a layer explicitly calibrated in the companion pilot to *remove* consensus items.

### 3.2 Confirmatory results — all three hypotheses supported

- **H1 (differentiation dominance).** Observed all-7 agreement: 63/129 (48.8%). Independence null: mean 72.0, 95% band [65, 79]. Observed falls below the band: p = .006, **Holm p = .0068**. Even on a battery one-sixth of which was *designed* to elicit consensus — a composition that raises both the observed count and the null, making the below-null result conservative — models agree less often than their pooled answer distributions predict: model-specific dispositions actively differentiate. (The null conditions on pooled marginals estimated from the same data; it is a descriptive baseline, not a causal model, §5.6.)
- **H2 (normative gradient).** Agreement on norm-transparent items: 24/28 = **85.7%**; on preference-pure items: 9/20 = **45.0%**; Δ = 40.7 percentage points, permutation p = .0034, **Holm p = .0068**. The denominators follow the pre-registered valid-item rule (PREREG §3: agreement is evaluated over items where all seven verdicts are non-NR; the resulting unequal n is a consequence of that frozen rule, not a post-hoc choice). Consensus concentrates precisely where a trained norm names an option. As a manipulation check beyond the registered test, the consensus winner on agreeing norm items was the designated `norm_option` in every case (24/24, descriptive); the four norm items that escaped consensus are released with their per-model verdicts (`norm_items_without_consensus.json`).
- **H3 (doxa replication).** On the full registered scope — all 120 re-administered habitus items, an NR-verdict item counting as non-doxa — the fresh classification agrees with Part A's at **κ = 0.649** (overlap 25; Part-A doxa 37, fresh doxa 30), permutation p ≤ 1/10,001, **Holm p = .0003**. On the 81 stable-verdict items (the frozen script's narrower scope, disclosed in §2.3) the agreement is κ = 0.711 (exploratory). Doxa is a stable, re-measurable property of items, not session noise — and the replication holds *across different session counts* (5 vs 3) and collection days.

### 3.3 Descriptive structure

The confirmed picture is normatively striped practice space: a doxa core where alignment training has erased position-taking (§3.2 H2; the companion pilot's zero-discrimination items — search before building, report failures with logs — were the same phenomenon observed at pilot scale, though those items were removed from the battery and are not re-tested here), a large differentiated region where models take stable, model-specific positions (companion study), and overall consensus *below* independence expectations because dispositions repel modal answers apart (H1). In Bourdieu's vocabulary: the field of machine practice has both a doxa and a space of position-takings, and the boundary between them is measurable and normatively structured.

## 4. Discussion

**For alignment measurement.** H2 offers a behavioral, label-frozen measure of *norm transparency*: the agreement rate of diverse models on an item estimates how unambiguously the item's option set encodes a trained norm. Items that were designed as norm-transparent but escaped consensus (4/28; ids 203, 209, 210, 219) are diagnostic — either the norm is contested across training regimes or the item leaks a competing consideration; their full per-model verdicts are in `norm_items_without_consensus.json`.

**For context owners and delegation.** The doxa region is where model choice does not matter — any aligned model converges — and the preference region is where it does (45% agreement means most preference items *split* the model population). A delegation policy can be calibrated accordingly: doxa-region tasks are safely portable across models; differentiated-region tasks require the habitus geometry (companion study) to predict what a given model will do. (The 45% figure conditions on stable verdicts; counting all 30 preference items, 9/30 = 30% reach consensus — the unconditional rate is lower still.)

**For the doxa concept.** The below-null H1 result sharpens the sociology: machine doxa is not mere homogenization. Alignment produces islands of enforced consensus inside a sea of *stronger-than-expected* differentiation. If anything, training simultaneously homogenizes the normative and diversifies the rest — a structure Bourdieu's framework describes but did not predict for machines.

**On enforcement continuity.** The companion study's protocol breach was caught by external adversarial review; this study's analogous event (an over-threshold session, plus an auto-triggered premature analysis) was caught by the authors before reporting and handled by the pre-registered rule. One study is an anecdote and two are a habit only if the habit survives adversarial review — which this manuscript, like its companion, underwent before release (three model judges, three families; record in `review_record/`).

## 5. Limitations

1. **Stimulus language** is Japanese only; the normative gradient may be language- and culture-dependent.
2. **Seven models, one date**; consensus among these deployments is not consensus of the model population. API subjects are moving targets.
3. **Valid-item filtering** (129/180): agreement is defined over items where every model produces a stable verdict; the 51 filtered items are disproportionately those that split some model's sessions, so the reported agreement rates condition on within-model stability. NR burden is also asymmetric across models (per-session NR up to 33 for one gemma4-26b session), so the filter removes items partly according to which model is unstable on them. An agreement measure robust to per-model NR is future work.
4. **3 sessions per model** bounds verdict reliability; H3's κ = 0.649 against a 5-session baseline suggests the classification is nonetheless stable.
5. **Norm labels were authored, not measured**: `norm_option` designations were fixed pre-collection by the authoring toolchain, which shares a vendor with two subjects; the 24/24 manipulation check partially mitigates but does not eliminate this circularity.
6. **Independence null** conditions on pooled marginals from the same data; it is a baseline, not a causal model.
7. No formal power analysis; with 28 + 20 valid labeled items, H2's effect had to be large to be detected — and was.
8. **Claude-subject provenance** is weaker than for the API-scripted subjects: block answers passed through an uninstrumented assembly path (§2.2), with no timing metadata; the frozen-runner prompts and stored mappings bound what the sessions saw, but not how responses returned. The post-hoc audit (`audit_claude_blocks.json`) establishes the retry guard would not have fired, not full provenance; the assembly path also accepts an answer line whose item id belongs to another block where the frozen parser would discard it, which occurred once in 1,080 Claude item-responses (claude-sonnet s1: item 41 answered in block 1, item 311 NR).
9. **Subjects are not independent draws**: two Gemma variants share an architecture and training pipeline and two Claude variants share a vendor, yet the all-7 agreement statistic and the independence null treat the seven verdicts as exchangeable. Family-correlated agreement inflates consensus counts, which makes H1's *below-null* result conservative, but the absolute rates (85.7%, 45.0%) should be read as rates among these seven deployments, not among seven independent training regimes. "Doxa" is used throughout as an operational label for all-subject modal agreement — a deliberately thin transplant of Bourdieu's concept, whose full sociological content (genesis, unthinkability, misrecognition) is not claimed to be measured.
10. **Exact replication of collection is not guaranteed**: the retry mitigation makes the number of API calls per block data-dependent, and API backends are stochastic; the deterministic seeds fix presentation, not responses. Replication targets the statistics, not the transcripts.

## 6. Conclusion

Machine practice space is normatively striped. Where alignment names an option, seven models from five vendor families agree almost always (85.7%); where it does not, they agree less often than chance-with-shared-marginals would predict (H1) — and both the consensus core and its boundary are stable, re-measurable objects (κ = 0.65 across studies on the registered scope). Doxa and habitus are not competing descriptions of LLM behavior but complementary halves of one measured geometry: what goes without saying, and what each model says in its own way.
